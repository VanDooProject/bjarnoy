# Garrison

- Land troops garrison in three places: longhouse, tower, beach camp. Fleets garrison in dock and beach camp, and in a
  longhouse that stands on sand next to the sea.
- Land troops cannot garrison in a dock. They go out onto the land tile with the best route to the nearest garrison spot
  (tower or longhouse) and march there on their own.
- An army that arrives home folds into the garrison. Leftover provisions go into the stock there: the settlement stock at a
  longhouse, tower or dock, the camp's piles at a beach camp ([troop-movement-range.md](troop-movement-range.md) §1a).
- A fleet's home is the garrison spot it last folded into. A fleet that reaches another settlement of its own with a dock, or
  a beach camp of its own, folds in there, and that becomes its new home.
- The round-trip food check of an army setting out from a beach camp is measured from the camp.
- Garrisons eat from the stock of their garrison spot, ships included.
- An army whose home is destroyed (for example a beach camp while its troops are out) gets the nearest garrison spot of its
  owner it can reach as its new home. If it can reach none, it stays where it is until its provisions run out, and then
  starves.

## Training, sending out, losing a spot

- Newly trained units join the nearest garrison spot that can hold them, measured from the building that trained them:
  land units the nearest tower or longhouse, ships the nearest dock (or a longhouse on sand next to the sea).
- An army can be put together from units of several garrison spots of one settlement. Each group leaves its own spot at
  once; they meet at the first hex their routes share. A group that gets there first waits, eating its provisions, and the
  army moves on together at the pace of its slowest unit. The walk to that hex and the wait count in the round-trip food
  check.
- The home of such an army is the settlement's garrison spot nearest the hex where the groups met.
- Sending out: the send picker counts the units of all spots together. It takes each unit type from the spots nearest the
  target first, then from the next-nearest. The unit picker shows which spot the units come from.
- Support: you can support a settlement only by choosing one of its garrison spots (its longhouse, a tower or a dock). The
  army joins that spot.
- When a tower or dock is destroyed, the units that survive in it march to the owner's nearest garrison spot that can hold
  them, and that spot becomes their home.

## Attacks

- Attacks are aimed at buildings, never at a settlement as a whole. Attacking a settlement means attacking a building in its realm;
  supporting a settlement means joining one of its garrisons. An attack on a building with a garrison (longhouse, tower,
  dock, beach camp) is fought by that building's garrison. An attack on any other building has no fight, unless an army stands
  on its hex; then that army fights.
- Loot comes from three buildings: a longhouse or a beach camp (from the whole stock) and a storage house (at most that house's
  own capacity). A storage house that was looted gives no loot for 24 h.
